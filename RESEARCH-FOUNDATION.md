# UI Rescue service — Research foundation

This pilot applies established usability and interface guidance to a bounded expert review. It does not create a new methodology, standards engine, certification program, or automatic compliance determination.

## Source classes

### Normative requirements and standards

- [W3C Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) is a normative web accessibility standard. Cite the exact success criterion and conformance context when a finding relies on it. A limited visual review does not establish WCAG conformance.
- [ISO 9241-210:2019](https://www.iso.org/standard/77520.html) provides requirements and recommendations for human-centred design activities across an interactive system lifecycle.
- [ISO 9241-110:2020](https://www.iso.org/standard/75258.html) provides general interaction principles and recommendations. ISO text may require licensed access; cite the standard and clause without reproducing protected text beyond permitted quotation.

Whether any standard is legally required depends on jurisdiction, contract, organization, and context. The operator does not make an unsupported legal-compliance claim.

### Professional and evaluation guidance

- [W3C WAI Evaluating Web Accessibility](https://www.w3.org/WAI/test-evaluate/) explains evaluation approaches and warns that no tool alone determines accessibility.
- [Nielsen Norman Group's 10 usability heuristics](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/) are broad rules of thumb, not specific conformance requirements.
- [Baymard's research methodology](https://baymard.com/research/methodology) is especially relevant to ecommerce and must not be generalized to every site or user population without qualification.
- [GOV.UK Service Manual: Make the service simple to use](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/service-standard/point-4-make-the-service-simple-to-use) provides public-service design guidance grounded in user needs and testing.
- [Google Lighthouse](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/lighthouse/overview) is an automated diagnostic tool; a report is evidence about that run, not a complete audit or outcome guarantee.
- [Google Web Vitals](https://web.dev/articles/vitals) defines measurable user-experience signals. Field and lab evidence, capture conditions, and metric versions must be identified.
- [FTC: Bringing Dark Patterns to Light](https://www.ftc.gov/reports/bringing-dark-patterns-light) describes deceptive-design concerns. It informs risk identification but does not turn this service into legal advice or an enforcement determination.

### Design hypotheses

An evaluator's proposed treatment is a hypothesis until supported by appropriate evidence. A heuristic concern, screenshot comparison, automated score, or preference is not proof of user difficulty, accessibility conformance, conversion lift, or revenue impact. Where user behavior matters, recommend suitable user research or measurement rather than asserting an outcome.

## Evidence and citation rules

For every material claim:

1. record the exact page/state, date, viewport, browser, and source revision when available;
2. link the screenshot, behavior check, metric output, or other observable evidence;
3. label facts, interpretations, hypotheses, and untested concerns separately;
4. cite the primary or authoritative source closest to the claim, including version or access date when the source may change;
5. state applicability limits, missing states, and checks not performed; and
6. never present a tool score, expert review, or partial check as certification, legal compliance, or complete coverage.

Use short quotations only when necessary. Prefer paraphrase plus a direct link, and respect source copyright and license terms.
